The Yellow Sea plain and ancient dogs. A, Modern land and coastlines, a uniform -120 m sea-level scenario, and selected dated dog records from East Asia. Gold marks modern bathymetric cells between -120 and 0 m; the dashed -120 m contour is a lowstand shoreline proxy. The solid -200 m contour is a present-day depth contour used as a shelf-margin guide, not a measured geomorphic shelf break or a dated palaeoshoreline. The base is NOAA ETOPO1 (2009), sampled at 2 arc-minute spacing from its native 1 arc-minute nodes, with Natural Earth 1:10 million modern land outlines. This is a contextual sea-level scenario, not a reconstruction corrected for glacial isostasy, tectonics, sedimentation, erosion or changing river systems. “Golden Plain” denotes the proposed source-region hypothesis, not an established domestication centre. Dog circles indicate nuclear-DNA evidence; open diamonds indicate mitochondrial-only records. Ages are rounded calibrated direct specimen dates; Harbin V0726 is river-bottom material without secure cultural context, and its point is the published collection locality. Purple outlines identify Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu as the historical island range of the Japanese wolf (Canis lupus hodophilax); they are regional context, not invented specimen positions or reconstructed continuous occupancy. B, Three early western Eurasian nuclear-DNA records, with the East Asian detail extent outlined. All records are selected examples, not a complete distribution or absence map. No migration direction is inferred by the map.
